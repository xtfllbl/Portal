# Preserve the effective operating schedule when interpreting uptime history

Current-rule reference, 2026-09-18: [the consolidated requirements](../prd/terminal-uptime.md) define the operative reporting rules. [ADR 0014](0014-backdate-offline-after-consecutive-missed-heartbeats.md) adds three-minute heartbeats with tolerance for one isolated miss; a second consecutive miss backdates Offline to the first missed heartbeat's expected time. Earlier reporting-policy amendments below are decision history and do not override the consolidated requirements.

Latest reporting-eligibility amendment, 2026-09-14 (Q30): a terminal-local day with zero successful Payment Service reports is No data and has no daily percentage; at least one successful report makes the day eligible for calculation. This supersedes the earlier blanket treatment of a wholly unreported day as Offline and the earlier collection-failure veto below. The reporting-day and effective-schedule decisions remain in force. Q31 confirms that a successful report is a valid heartbeat and therefore Online evidence; there is no terminal-reported Offline. The prototype now applies this eligibility rule; production heartbeat ingestion remains outside this implementation. See the [data-integrity review](../design/terminal-uptime/data-integrity-2026-09-14.md).

Daily Payment Service uptime is assessed only within elapsed Operating Hours and grouped by the terminal's local calendar day, splitting overnight periods across the dates they overlap. Schedule changes affect only time from their effective moment onward; historical periods retain the Operating Hours effective at the time rather than being reinterpreted through the latest schedule. This prevents a later closure or shorter schedule from erasing previously counted downtime, at the cost of retaining effective schedule versions; Store changes affect terminals following that Store but do not replace individual terminal overrides.

The initial 2026-09-11 reporting policy required full observation before assigning a percentage. Its missing-data reporting restriction is superseded by the 2026-09-14 decision below. Unknown Payment Service periods remain distinct from confirmed online or offline periods and must not be presented as fully observed uptime. Non-operating and future periods do not participate in the uptime calculation; raw all-day observations remain available in the detail view. These decisions define intended behavior, not an implemented production telemetry or scheduling service.

On 2026-09-14, the user confirmed a conservative reporting policy: elapsed expected-but-unreported time counts as not online, while the original evidence remains unknown and is listed separately as No report. A known platform collection failure or conflicting reports makes that day Data unavailable instead of assigning a numeric rate or blaming the terminal. Future and non-operating time remain excluded. In progress marks only the terminal-local day that has not ended; it is independent of missing reports. Day health uses the unrounded rate: green at or above 95%, yellow from 90% to below 95%, red below 90%. This permits useful operational comparison without treating a green day as proof of zero downtime, and preserves the ability to correct history using authoritative observations and the effective historical schedule. See the [confirmed revision](../design/terminal-uptime/revision-2026-09-14.md).

A later clarification on 2026-09-14 supersedes the separate customer-facing Confirmed offline / No report categories: both become **Unreachable**, meaning expected service communication was absent, with no diagnosis of network or machine failure. Their already-combined duration and the uptime denominator remain unchanged. The prototype may preserve legacy source fields for traceability, but the duration cards, timeline, detail rows and filters use one service category. Known platform collection failures remain Data unavailable. The user selected Unreachable in Q25; see the [layout and status refinement](../design/terminal-uptime/refinement-2026-09-14.md).

The subsequently confirmed Q26–Q29 UI refinement renames the customer-facing Unreachable category to **Offline**, retaining exactly the same absence-of-communication meaning and calculation. A day without an assessable rate displays `—`, with a compact No data or Starts at HH:mm explanation; it remains distinct from a measured 0%. The previous labels above describe decision history. See the [confirmed UI refinement](../design/terminal-uptime/ui-polish-2026-09-14.md).

On 2026-09-15, the user confirmed retirement of Special dates from activation of the simplified editor. Active Store schedules and valid terminal overrides containing exceptions receive a new weekly-only version at activation; preceding versions remain intact. This removes hidden future exceptions without rewriting prior uptime. The prototype activates this migration once per browser data set when it first loads the new version; production must use an authoritative server activation instant. Change history is removed from the editor, while internal versions and audit remain necessary for these historical guarantees.
